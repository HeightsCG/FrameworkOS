## What are the three ways to sell photos online, and which fits your goals?

The three ways to sell photos online each fit a different goal, so match your goal to one model before picking a tool. The model decides the pricing, the platform type, and how much of each sale you keep.

### Goal 1: passive stock licensing

Stock licensing means you upload once and earn per download, for as long as the photo sells. Volume matters more than any single sale, so stock fits photographers building a library over months. Expect low per-download payouts and heavy competition.

### Goal 2: prints and print-on-demand

Print-on-demand lets you sell physical products with no audience required. The printer handles fulfillment; you set a markup per print. Margins are thin per unit, so prints work best as a supplement, not a main income.

### Goal 3: photo sets, bundles and PPV for a fan audience

Photo sets suit creators who already have fans paying memberships or PPV on OnlyFans, Fanvue, Patreon or Beacons. Selling photo sets, bundles and tiers direct to those fans is usually the highest-earning path, because you control the take rate instead of accepting a 10-20% platform fee. Creator Link Studio falls short if you want stock distribution or physical print fulfillment; neither is handled here.

## Where can you sell photos online: stock sites, print-on-demand, or your own website?

You can sell photos online on stock sites, through print-on-demand, or from your own page, and each trades reach against control.

### Stock and marketplace trade-offs

Stock sites handle licensing and buyer traffic for you, but you compete with millions of images and earn cents per download. You also lose the customer relationship: no email, no repeat buyer, no upsell to a bundle or PPV set.

### Print-on-demand trade-offs

Print-on-demand turns your photos into posters, mugs and shirts with zero inventory. The catch is margins: the printer's base cost comes off the top, so a $30 print might leave you $8. Print-on-demand works best as a passive add-on, not your main income.

### Your own page: control and take rate

Creators who already earn on OnlyFans, Fanvue, Patreon or Beacons have the audience stock sites will never give them. Selling photo sets direct means the buyer is yours, the pricing is yours, and the take rate is the number that matters. A 15% platform fee costs you $150 on every $1,000 of photo sales, and card processing comes out on every platform.

Your own page also replaces the stitched-together stack: link-in-bio, scheduler and store in one place, with bundles, membership tiers and free trials built in. The honest trade-off: you drive the traffic, and there is no marketplace discovery to fall back on.

## How do you price your photos for licenses, prints, and direct sales?

Photo pricing works differently for each model: licenses price by usage, prints by margin, and direct sales by what your fans already pay.

### Pricing licenses

Stock licensing rewards volume, not glamour. Illustrative starting points: $5-$15 for a small web-resolution license, $25-$75 for extended commercial use, $100+ for exclusive rights. Price exclusives at 5-10x your standard rate, because the buyer is removing the photo from your catalog entirely.

### Pricing prints

Print pricing works backwards from margin. If a print-on-demand lab charges you $12 for an 8x10 and you sell at $35, you keep roughly $23 before platform fees. Anchor prints at 2.5-3x your landed cost, and offer two or three sizes so buyers self-select instead of haggling.

### Pricing sets, bundles and PPV

Direct sales should match what fans already pay for memberships. If your subscription runs $10-$15/month, price a single PPV photo set at $5-$12, a bundle of three sets at $15-$25, and a "best of" archive at $30-$50. A free teaser set converts lurkers into buyers; a discounted trial tier does the same for memberships. ## How do you prepare and keyword photos so buyers actually find them?

Photos sell better when you curate before you upload and caption for the buyer you have: keywords for stock, teasers for fans.

### Prep and curation

Curation starts before you upload anything. Pick your strongest 10-20 shots per collection; a bloated catalog makes every photo look cheaper. Export at full resolution in JPEG (and TIFF or RAW if the platform accepts it), keep color profiles consistent, and check that nothing is cropped oddly at different aspect ratios.

For fan audiences, add watermarked or heavily blurred previews on your PPV items so the paid version stays the only uncensored copy.

### Keywording for stock vs teasing for fans

Stock buyers search by subject and concept, so keyword literally: mood, setting, clothing, season, and use case. Fan audiences behave differently. A teaser caption like "full set, 40 photos, in my link-in-bio" outperforms keyword stuffing; tease the pay-per-view drop, then post the bundle to your store.
