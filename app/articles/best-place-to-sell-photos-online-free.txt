## What does 'free to sell' actually mean: listing fees, commissions, and payout minimums compared?

Free to sell is not something any platform offers. They are free to list. The costs show up in three places: the cut taken from each sale, the minimum balance before you can withdraw to your bank, and withdrawal or processing fees on top.

### Commission: the real cost of a 'free' listing

The commission is the number that matters. OnlyFans takes 20% of each fan payment, per the [OnlyFans terms of service](https://onlyfans.com/terms). Patreon takes a 10% platform fee on pages published after August 4, 2025, before payment processing, per [Patreon's creator fees overview](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/11111747095181-Creator-fees-overview). A "free" listing at a 20% take rate costs you $200 on every $1,000 in PPV sales, every month.

### Payout thresholds and withdrawal options

A payout threshold only hurts when the minimum delays your money. Say you earn $60 a month and the minimum payout is $100: your cash sits on the platform. Check the payout schedule, not just the minimum. On Creator Link Studio the payout minimum is $25, and you request a payout to your bank when you want one.

| Commission per sale | Take rate on PPV and memberships | Compounds on every sale, forever |
| Payout minimum | Balance needed before withdrawal | Traps small earners |

## Which platforms let you sell photos with zero upfront cost and no monthly plan?

Platforms that are free to list charge nothing monthly, but every one still takes a cut or holds your money until a payout threshold clears. Check each platform's payout and withdrawal documentation before committing, because thresholds and cash-out timing vary more than take rates do.

### Marketplaces for one-off sales

OnlyFans and Fanvue let you list PPV photo sets and bundles with no setup fee and no monthly plan. You price per message or per post, and the platform takes its percentage at checkout. Patreon works the same way for membership tiers built around photo drops, with a free trial option for new fans.

### Storefronts and link-in-bio stores

Beacons and Crownerr give you a free storefront page where fans buy sets directly, replacing the stitched-together stack of link-in-bio plus scheduler plus store. Creator Link Studio works differently: you can build your page on the Free plan, but selling pay-per-view posts, bundles and membership tiers starts on a paid plan. The honest trade-off: a newer storefront means fewer built-in discovery features than the big membership platforms, so it works best if you already bring traffic from social.

## Should a hobbyist with no audience use a marketplace or build their own free storefront?

A hobbyist photographer with no audience should use a marketplace. Without followers, discovery is your bottleneck, and a storefront with zero traffic sells zero photos. A marketplace brings browsers who are already searching for photos; your job is tags, titles, and volume, not marketing.

A free storefront still costs you nothing but setup time, so there is a case for running both: list on a marketplace for discovery, and keep a home base like a link-in-bio page so buyers who do find you have somewhere to land. Creator Link Studio's publishing tools help you build that home base, but be honest about the tradeoff: we do not send you an audience. If you have no following, our storefront alone will not sell photos.

Your own storefront becomes the priority once you have engaged fans who click your links. Until then, go where the traffic already is.

## Where do stock and fine-art photographers earn differently on free platforms?

Stock and fine-art photographers earn differently because the license type decides the platform. Royalty stock pays fractions per download and only works at volume; per-print pricing pays fat margins on few sales. Pick based on which math fits your catalogue.

### Stock: volume economics

Microstock pays a royalty per download. Say a photo earns $0.33 a sale: it needs roughly 3,000 downloads to clear $1,000. Volume like that only works with hundreds of images live and steady keyword work. Free-to-list stock sites fit you if you shoot in bulk and treat payouts as a slow drip, not a paycheck.

### Fine art and prints: margin economics

Print sales flip the trade-off: one $80 framed print beats 240 stock downloads at $0.33 each. You need fewer sales but you carry the audience-building yourself, since per-print shops rarely send buyers to you.

Creator Link Studio sells digital photo sets, not physical prints, and bundles or pay-per-view posts let you price a limited digital run like fine art rather than a commodity. The honest gap: it has no stock marketplace, so there is no built-in buyer flow for volume shooters. ## I already have a following, so where should I sell my photos?

A creator who already has an audience does not need discovery. Your job is keeping more of each sale, so the take rate matters more than the listing fee.

## What do you give up by selling on a free platform versus a paid plan?

Selling on a free platform trades money for control. On most free plans you keep less per sale, carry the platform's branding on your storefront, get slower payouts, and never own the customer relationship: emails, purchase history and payment details belong to the platform, so migrating means starting your list from zero. Licensing is the hidden trade-off too. Stock-style sales are almost always non-exclusive, meaning the same photo can keep selling to other buyers after you've sold it once.

Running our own plans through the same criteria: Creator Link Studio's Free plan lets you set up your page, but you need a paid plan before you can sell. Your page sits on our domain until you connect a custom domain, which comes with Studio. If you're earning steadily on OnlyFans, Fanvue, Patreon or Beacons and want to keep more of each sale, a paid plan buys back that control.

## Which free platform should you pick based on your portfolio size and audience?

Your portfolio size and audience give four profiles and four picks. If you have under 50 photos and no audience, list on Beacons as a free storefront and post the link everywhere you already hang out. If you sell PPV and memberships today on OnlyFans or Fanvue, stay put for distribution but run a Creator Link Studio page alongside it, so your own fans buy from your page instead of a stitched stack.

If you have a real following but no paid product yet, start on Patreon with a low tier and a free trial, then move bundle sales to your own page once recurring income is steady. If you are a print or stock shooter with a deep portfolio, a free marketplace listing is your fastest first sale. For every profile, the switch comes when your audience buys from you directly: that is when your own store and a bank payout pay off.

## FAQ

### Where can I sell my photos online for free with no commission?

Nowhere. Every platform that lets you list photos without upfront cost takes a cut of each sale instead. OnlyFans takes 20%, Patreon takes 10% on new pages before processing fees, and storefronts like Beacons charge on transactions too. The closest to zero commission is running your own checkout, which means bringing your own traffic.

### Do I need my own website to sell photos online?

No. Stock and photo marketplaces bring browsers searching for photos, which matters most when you have no audience. A website or storefront earns you more per sale, but only if you can point followers at it. The common setup is both: sell on a marketplace for discovery and keep a link-in-bio page as a home base.

### How much money can a beginner make selling photos online?

There is no reliable fixed figure; it depends on volume and profile. Stock shooters earn small royalties per download, so volume matters more than any single photo. PPV sellers with a following earn more per sale. Hobbyists with no audience should expect slow starts.

### What is the best free app to sell photos?

The best free app depends on your profile. For PPV photo sets and memberships, OnlyFans and Fanvue list with no setup fee. For membership tiers around photo drops, Patreon works well. For a free storefront page fans buy from directly, Beacons or Crownerr. Match the app to how you sell: one-off sets, recurring tiers, or direct storefront purchases.

### Can I sell the same photos on multiple platforms?

Usually yes, but check licensing terms first. Stock-style sales are almost always non-exclusive, so the same photo can keep selling to other buyers after a sale, and platforms generally do not stop you from listing elsewhere. Read each platform's terms before uploading, because exclusivity clauses, if any, decide whether multi-platform selling is allowed.
